    Advance Sales Analysis¶

    Access

    Advance Sales is available to Admin and Theater users. Box Office users do not have access. Theater users see only productions belonging to their theaters, and only their own theaters in the theater picker.

    Navigation: Admin Menu > Analysis > Advance Sales tab

    Shortcut from a production

    On any production's admin page (Productions > [Production Name]), click the Advance Sales button next to Audience Analysis. It opens the form with that production's theater and season selected and the comparison set to Production.

    What it answers¶

    Advance Sales looks at when tickets were bought relative to the performance they were bought for:

    • How far ahead do patrons plan? What share of a show's tickets are bought the same day, the week before, or a month or more out?
    • Is that changing over time? Compare seasons for one company or for the whole building.
    • Does it differ from company to company? Compare theaters side by side.
    • What does a run look like week by week, and how did performances added after opening sell compared with the original run?

    A related Pace column on each production's performance list uses the same history to project where an upcoming performance is heading. See Pace.

    Running the analysis¶

    Field What it does
    Seasons Tick one or more seasons. The two most recent seasons are ticked on your first visit. At least one is required.
    Theaters A typeahead picker. Type part of a theater's name or a tag; choose a theater to add it, or an All theaters tagged ... entry to add every member of that tag in one click. Remove rows individually or with Remove all. Leave the list empty to include every theater you can see. If you have access to only one theater, the picker is not shown and the analysis uses that theater.
    Compare by What each line on the chart represents: a Season, a Theater, or a Production.
    Tickets Both draws a single-ticket line and a pass line per group; Single tickets or Passes draws one line per group. See What counts.
    Include theaters with sales recorded externally Shown only when such a theater is in your list. Off by default. See Sales recorded externally.

    Results are bookmarkable: the address bar carries the full selection, so you can share or revisit a run. Changing any filter clears the results until you click Run analysis again.

    Reading the results¶

    The chart: share of final sales in hand¶

    Each line answers, for one series, "how much of this group's eventual ticket total had already been sold N days before the performance?" The x-axis runs from 60 days out to the day of the show, and every line climbs to 100% at Same day. A line that sits high on the left belongs to an audience that plans ahead; a line that stays low until the last week belongs to a walk-up audience. The steepest stretch of a line is the window in which most of its tickets sold.

    The table: sales by window¶

    The table under the chart shows the same sales a different way. Each column is the share of the series' final sales bought in that window -- Same day, 1-2d, 3-6d, 7-13d, 14-29d and 30+d -- so a row adds up to 100%. Read the chart for the shape of the curve and the table for how much landed in each window.

    Column Meaning
    Tickets Tickets counted in the series.
    Upcoming For a series still in progress, how many of its performances with tickets sold have not yet played, out of its total. A dash for a finished series.
    Same day ... 30+d Share of final sales bought that many days before the performance.

    Series in progress

    A performance that hasn't played yet has no final total: only its early buyers exist so far. A series that includes such performances is labelled (in progress), drawn with a dashed line, and listed with an Upcoming count. Its curve will move toward same-day as the run completes. Compare finished seasons for a fair read, and treat an in-progress season as provisional.

    Small series

    A line built from a handful of tickets is a sample, not a pattern. Check the Tickets column before drawing conclusions from a flat or stepped line.

    Week by week¶

    Below the comparison, one collapsible panel per production breaks its run into weeks counted from the production's first playing date (first preview, press opening or opening, whichever comes first). Expand a panel to see:

    Column Meaning
    Week Week 1 is the first seven days of the run; Week before is the seven days before it; Earlier collects anything before that.
    Starts The first date of that week.
    Perfs Performances in the week with tickets sold.
    Added later Performances that were first activated after the run had started -- typically an extension. The panel title also shows the production's total as N added after opening.
    Tickets Tickets sold for performances in the week.
    Avg lead Average days between purchase and performance, weighted by tickets.
    Same day Share bought on the day of the performance.
    >14d Share bought more than two weeks ahead.

    A N upcoming label on a week means that many of its performances have not played yet.

    Extensions

    Added performances sell to a different crowd. Across the building, extension weeks run roughly 40% same-day against about 25% in an opening week, so an added week leans on late buyers and word of mouth. The Added later column lets you see how a specific extension sold relative to the original run.

    What counts¶

    Only Active performances are counted; Private and Inactive performances are left out.

    • Tickets are seat-holding, non-complimentary tickets on sold ticket orders, the same basis as house counts. Comps are left out, as are add-on items that don't occupy a seat.
    • The sale date is the original purchase. An exchanged or split order is traced back to the first order, so moving a ticket to a different night doesn't change when the patron decided to buy.
    • Lead time is the number of days between that purchase date and the performance date. A purchase on the day of the show is Same day.
    • Passes are tickets paid with a flex pass whose offer is tied to a specific theater or festival -- the kind of pass sold as a subscription. Tickets paid with any other pass, with a membership, or in cash or card count as single tickets. Purchases of passes and memberships themselves are not ticket sales and are never counted.

    Sales recorded externally¶

    Some companies sell through their own box office and have their tickets keyed into Stagemgr afterwards. For those theaters the order date is the data-entry date, not the purchase date, so their lead times are meaningless and would distort any comparison. Tick Sales recorded externally on the theater form to keep such a theater out of Advance Sales by default. The theater's own production pages still show it, and the form offers a checkbox to include it in a run deliberately.

    Pace on the performance list¶

    On a production's admin page, the performance list has a Pace column for upcoming performances. It projects where each performance is heading from the theater's own history: if this house typically has 60% of a performance's final sales in hand a week out, and a performance a week away has sold 30 tickets, the projection is about 50 tickets. The cell reads ~50 (48% of capacity); hover over it for the basis.

    Reading Meaning
    ~N (P% of capacity) Projected final count and its share of the production's capacity.
    Too early The theater typically has under 10% in hand this far out, so a projection would be noise.
    -- The performance has played, is inactive, or there is not enough history to project from.

    The history is the theater's earlier seasons. A theater with fewer than 1,000 tickets of history uses the building-wide curve instead; the hover text says so. The Pace column is not affected by in-progress seasons, because it only ever looks at seasons before the production's own.

    Note

    Pace is a projection from typical behaviour, not a forecast of this show. A strong review, a snowstorm, or a late press push will move a performance off its house's usual curve.
